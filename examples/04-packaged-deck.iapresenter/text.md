# Ship the Rough Draft
## Why finished is the enemy of shipped

This is the output contract in practice: a real `.iapresenter` package (`text.md` + `info.json`), a deck that follows the corpus rules, and a topic anyone can map onto their own work. I open by naming the fear — that nothing is ever good enough to release.

---

## We don't ship because we're afraid

The honest reason isn't quality. It's exposure. Shipping means someone can finally judge the thing, so we keep polishing to delay the verdict.

	Polishing feels like progress.

	It's usually just hiding.

I let that second line sit before I move on.

---

### "Real artists ship."

#### Steve Jobs said it because it's hard

A quote does the persuading here so I don't have to. I attribute it, then connect it to the room: the people we admire shipped before they felt ready, every time.

---

## What waiting actually costs

Delay isn't neutral. Every week the draft sits, it gets more expensive to change and less likely to ever go out.

	1. You forget why you started
	2. The market moves
	3. The fear compounds

Each point is one beat. I don't read them — I expand on them out loud.

---

## Finished vs. shipped

Two different things, and we confuse them constantly. This is the turn of the whole talk.

	Finished
	A story you tell yourself

	Shipped
	A fact other people can react to

The side-by-side makes the contrast land without me explaining the layout.

---

### The rough draft is the strategy

#### Not a compromise — the plan

Shipping early isn't lowering the bar. It's how you find the real bar, because feedback on a real thing beats speculation about a perfect one.

---

## What "rough" should still clear

Rough doesn't mean broken. There's a floor, and it's lower than you think but it's not zero.

	- It works for the main case
	- It won't embarrass you in a year
	- You can change it after feedback

Three checks, not thirty. If it clears these, it goes out.

---

# Pick one thing. Ship it this week.

I close on the action, not a summary. One thing, a deadline, and the promise that the version after it will be better precisely because this one went out.
