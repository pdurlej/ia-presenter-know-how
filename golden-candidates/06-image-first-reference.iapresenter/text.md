## We measure the wrong clock

We report average response time. The customer experiences something else entirely: the gap between asking and knowing.

	Average response time: what we watch.

	Time-to-certainty: what they feel.

Those two numbers drift apart, and the second one is the one that ends relationships.

## What silence actually costs us

Now the exact numbers, because this is the one slide where the grid *is* the message. Flush-left, never TAB-indented, or it renders as raw code.

| Signal | Today | After the change |
|--------|-------|------------------|
| First human reply | 4h 10m | 6m (automated ack) |
| Reopened tickets | 31% | 18% |
| "Did you get this?" follow-ups | 1 in 4 | 1 in 20 |

The middle row is the one I linger on: fewer reopens means we answered the real question the first time.

## Three moves, in order

Order matters here, so it is a sequence, not a bullet list.

	1. Acknowledge in minutes, automatically
	2. Tell them what happens next, and when
	3. Only then work on speed

Most teams start at three. Starting at one is cheaper and moves the number that matters.
